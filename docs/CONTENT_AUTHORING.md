# Lesson design

CSQuest lessons are designed to teach a clear concept through explanation, a complete worked example, interactive exploration and practice. Every lesson should make sense on its own and identify what the learner is expected to understand.

## Teaching standards

- Introduce unfamiliar terms before using them in a question.
- Explain the problem, inputs, assumptions and units explicitly.
- Use tables for mappings, ordered steps for processes and labeled diagrams for structure.
- Give complete worked solutions, including why a plausible wrong answer fails.
- Mix recall, classification, manipulation, tracing and application where appropriate.
- Check arithmetic, logical rules, boundary cases and model limitations.

Interactive examples should respond to real input changes. Static figures do not need playback controls. Multi-step models support stepping, pause, restart and reduced motion. Exploratory predictions remain separate from graded answers and do not award XP.

## Accessible learning

Keep explanations readable on phones and desktops, in both themes. Controls need keyboard access, visible focus and adequate touch targets. Wide tables and diagrams scroll inside their own containers. Avoid relying on color alone to communicate state.

Lesson identifiers, scoring rules and saved progress remain stable across editorial updates. Optional workshops and coding labs do not block normal learning progression. Publication and verification are described in [adding subjects](ADDING_SUBJECTS.md).
