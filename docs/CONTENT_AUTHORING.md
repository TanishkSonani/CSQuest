# Course material rules

Use the supplied recordings and completed transcripts as the sources for subject matter. Verify arithmetic and logic when turning them into original examples. Keep transcript passages and timestamps as separate source references.

Questions, lesson titles, explanations, and teaching steps must teach the topic directly. Do not ask what a course, video, instructor, or lecture mentioned, or frame facts as “in this lecture.” State any architectural model, bit width, signal polarity, or other assumption explicitly in the problem.

Opening flip cards must be complete worked examples. The front supplies the problem and all required inputs. The back explains the result in a form suited to the topic: mappings as tables, processes as ordered steps, truth tables as rows, and calculations with their units. A card must make sense on its own without having attempted the practice puzzle.
