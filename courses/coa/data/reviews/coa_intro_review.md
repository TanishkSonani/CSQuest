# Introductory material: partial review

This is a partial review of the first two minutes of video 000 and pages 6, 8, 9, 10, and 11 of `coa/1.pdf`. It is not a review of the complete lecture or course.

The audio introduces computer organization and architecture as the hardware foundation supporting computer applications. It frames the subject around how a computer operates, how it is designed, and how it is built.

The slides connect this knowledge to hardware engineering and software engineering, including application performance and efficiency. They illustrate architecture through the machine language the computer accepts: supported instructions, instruction encoding, data representation, and processing logic. Organization concerns assembling hardware components and arranging their communication to implement that functionality.

The lecturer uses a person-engineer-computer diagram to explain the relationship between the instructions a user can express and the hardware that executes them. A light-switch analogy separates an operation (turn on) from its operand (the light). An instruction sketch then represents both using bits. The mnemonic examples shown include ADD, MULT, SUB, LOAD, and STORE. These are teaching illustrations; they are not a specification of a real processor's instruction encoding.

The displayed syllabus also mentions instruction pipelining, hazards, and interrupt/DMA I/O. Those topics are not identifiable in the 35 video filenames inventoried here; whether they appear within these recordings remains unverified.
